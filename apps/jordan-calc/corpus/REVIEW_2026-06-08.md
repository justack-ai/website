## Detail

### 1. R. v. Vrbanic, 2026 SCC 19 (reasons 2026-05-29) — complexity exception
The majority (8-1) allowed the Crown's appeal and set aside the stay. It set out a structured three-part test for the complexity exception and held that "particularly complex" does **not** mean "rare," and that more cases may now engage the exception. On remedy, the Court **declined** to add a residual discretion — a stay remains the automatic remedy for a proven breach — but expressly left the door open (paras. 80-82) to reconsidering the stay-only rule in a future case.
- **Effect on the tool:** This broadens the complexity exception (the corpus predicted `expand_deduction` — confirmed). The `complexity_exception` issue should be updated to reflect the new test and the "not rare" point, and Vrbanic should become controlling authority for it. The remedy framework is unchanged for now (stay remains automatic), but the open door should be noted.

### 2. R. v. Jacques-Taylor, 2026 SCC 20 (reasons 2026-05-29) — co-accused delay; Hanan
The majority (6-3) allowed the Crown's appeal. It held that delay caused by a co-accused in a joint trial can be a **discrete exceptional circumstance** (adopting the four-part *Tran* test), and again that "exceptional" does not mean "rare." Critically, the Court **expressly declined to decide** the *Hanan* contextual-approach question on initial date-setting — so the feared restriction on defence-delay attribution did **not** materialise; that question remains open.
- **Effect on the tool:** This is the one the corpus flagged as potentially affecting all delay attribution. The actual outcome is narrower and, for the tool, mostly relevant to the **multi-accused** path — there is now SCC authority that co-accused delay can be deducted as an exceptional circumstance. The general `defence_delay_attribution` corpus does not need to be restricted (Hanan untouched). Consider whether the `multi_accused` warning should now point to Jacques-Taylor rather than only disclaiming support.

### 3. R. v. R.B.-C., SCC file 41677 — still pending
Heard 2026-01-16; judgment reserved as of 2026-06-08; no neutral citation yet. The certified questions are the test for unreasonable delay in the **sentencing** phase and the appropriate remedy. The Court of Appeal had used a five-month presumptive ceiling for post-conviction delay and imposed a sentence reduction rather than a stay. This is the case to watch for whether the SCC will endorse a non-stay remedy — the thread Vrbanic left open.
- **Action:** Update watch metadata: status `leave_granted` → `heard_under_reserve`; `last_checked` 2026-06-08.

If proclaimed, it would require considering alternatives to a stay before ordering one (a `restrict_remedy` direction) — but it is not yet law, so the tool's stay-as-remedy output remains correct.
